# 1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

LABscape is an immersive, gamified, interactive virtual science laboratory where students and curious learners can perform experiments in Chemistry, Physics and Mathematics through realistic 2D/3D simulations, interactive animations, sound effects, visual reactions, challenges, quizzes and an open experimental playground.

Instead of simply reading about experiments in textbooks or watching someone else perform them, users can actually perform experiments virtually.

Users can:

- Pick up test tubes, beakers, flasks, pipettes, droppers and other laboratory equipment.
- Pour chemicals and solutions into containers.
- Control the quantity, speed, angle and manner of pouring.
- Add liquids drop by drop when required.
- Add substances slowly, quickly or in controlled portions, depending on the actual experimental procedure.
- Follow the correct order of adding chemicals.
- Stir, shake, swirl, heat, cool, measure and mix substances.
- Observe chemical reactions, colour changes, precipitates, bubbles, gas formation and temperature changes.
- Explore molecular structures and understand how reactants transform into products.
- Build electrical circuits, connect wires, change voltage and observe current.
- Experiment with gravity, friction, momentum, pressure, optics and waves.
- Manipulate mathematical objects, graphs, equations and geometric constructions.
- Make mistakes, receive hints, retry experiments and understand scientific principles.
- Complete quizzes, earn XP, unlock achievements and progress through laboratory levels.
- Enter an Open Playground to explore scientifically supported combinations and experiments.
- Experience real-world emergency simulations, such as a gas appliance fire, chemical spills and electrical emergencies.
- Interact with an AI science assistant that explains observations and helps troubleshoot experiments.

**The central idea:** Make science feel like playing a highly interactive simulation game rather than studying from a textbook.

LABscape is not simply a collection of virtual experiments. It is a complete virtual science ecosystem combining:

1. Virtual laboratory simulations.
2. Realistic experimental procedures.
3. Gamified learning.
4. Open-ended scientific exploration.
5. AI-powered tutoring.
6. Scientific safety education.
7. Progress tracking and analytics.
8. Social and competitive learning.
9. Freemium and subscription-based access.

# 9. REALISTIC EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES — CORE FEATURE

Experiments must NOT simply be "pour two liquids together and show an animation".

**The virtual experiment should replicate the actual laboratory procedure as closely as scientifically and technically practical.** The same substances can produce different outcomes depending on quantities, concentrations, temperature, order of addition, speed of addition, mixing method and other conditions. LABscape must account for these factors whenever relevant.

## 9.1 Controlled Pouring and Liquid Addition

Users control: pouring speed, pouring angle, volume added, number of drops, duration of addition, continuous vs intermittent pouring, dropwise addition, addition in small portions, gradual addition, addition at a controlled rate.

- Dropwise protocol → user physically operates a dropper or burette and adds individual drops.
- Slow addition → user tilts the container carefully and controls the flow.
- Faster addition permitted → simulation allows a faster pour.

The system must not force every experiment to use the same pouring mechanism.

## 9.2 Order of Addition

For every experiment, define:

- Which substance is added first / next.
- Whether a substance must be added gradually.
- Whether mixing occurs between additions.
- Whether the mixture needs to settle.
- Whether a step requires waiting.
- Whether heating or cooling is required.
- Whether measurements must be taken before proceeding.

If steps are done in the wrong order, respond per the validated experiment model — do NOT auto-show the success result. Instead:

- Identify the procedural mistake.
- Explain why the order matters.
- Show the resulting observation if the alternative procedure is scientifically modelled.
- Offer a hint.
- Allow restart.

## 9.3 Mixing Techniques

Gentle stirring · continuous stirring · swirling · shaking · dropwise mixing · controlled addition · allowing to settle · heating while stirring (where appropriate) · cooling before next step (where appropriate).

Each experiment specifies the correct method; the animation reflects the selected technique (gentle swirling looks different from vigorous stirring).

## 9.4 Measurement Accuracy

Measure liquid volume · read the meniscus · measure mass · record temperature · measure time · adjust concentrations · use appropriate instruments.

The system recognises measurement errors and shows their effect when the experiment model supports it.

## 9.5 Timing and Temperature Control

Timed reactions · heating and cooling · temperature indicators · reaction duration · waiting periods · time-dependent observations · temperature-dependent outcomes where modelled.

## 9.6 Realistic Procedural Evaluation

The engine monitors: correct equipment, substance, quantity, order, addition speed, mixing method, temperature conditions, timing, measurement technique.

User receives a **procedural accuracy score**, e.g.:

- Correct equipment: 100%
- Correct sequence: 100%
- Correct volume: 90%
- Correct pouring technique: 80%
- Correct observations: 100%

The system distinguishes between an incorrect procedure and incorrect scientific understanding.

## 9.7 Adaptive Procedural Hints

- Too fast when dropwise required: "Slow down! This step requires controlled dropwise addition. Try using the dropper instead of pouring the entire liquid at once."
- Wrong quantity: "Your measured volume is outside the specified range. Check the measuring cylinder and adjust the amount."
- Wrong order: "This step should be performed after the previous solution has been prepared. Review the procedure before continuing."

Goal: teach not only the scientific result but also correct laboratory technique.

## 11.2 Interactive Chemical Pouring

Example interaction:

1. User selects a reagent bottle.
2. Picks it up.
3. Moves it over the test tube.
4. Tilts the bottle.
5. Controls the pouring speed.
6. Liquid flows into the test tube.
7. The volume indicator updates.
8. The system checks the amount added.
9. The system checks whether the correct procedure was followed.
10. If a supported reaction occurs, the animation engine starts.

Liquid should have realistic: flowing motion, transparency, surface level, colour, refraction, meniscus, bubbles, particles, precipitate formation.
